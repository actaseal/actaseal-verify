What you are paying more for

Workflow systems store the evidence and record what happened to it. Ask one of them to prove a workpaper was locked before the documentation completion date and the proof is the vendor's own database saying so. That is an assertion, and it is only as good as the vendor's word and the vendor's continued existence.

What ActaSeal produces instead is a signed artifact. A PolicyDecisionReceipt binds the policy, the action, the ledger entry and the terms document in force at decision time, and it verifies offline, from a public key, by a party who has never run ActaSeal and has no reason to trust us. The named artifacts that follow from that -- a PCAOB AS 1215 documentation-completion archive lock, an FRE 902(13)/(14) self-authenticating certification package, an FRCP 37(e) preservation record, a 21 CFR 11.10(e) audit-trail review record -- are the artifacts themselves, not reports about them.

The usual reason to discount a small vendor is the question of what happens if it disappears. Our answer is written down and testable rather than promised: the verifier is Apache-2.0 and published on its own, the packet format is frozen per schema version and that freeze is enforced by the verifier itself, every packet embeds its own trust root so no registry lookup is ever required, and the graveyard sets out what happened to the products that tried this before us. Evidence you produce today outlives this company by construction. That is a reason to pay more for it, not less.

What is not included, stated plainly

No SOC 2 report. ActaSeal has not been audited under SOC 2 Type II. If your vendor qualification requires one by name, we do not meet it today and will say so in writing.

No hosted service. Every tier is deployed inside your own infrastructure. There is no multi-customer service holding your evidence, which also means there is no hosted uptime commitment to buy.

Multi-tenant isolation is enforced by default-deny scope checks in code and has not yet been reviewed by an independent tester. If that review is a condition of your deployment, say so and it goes in the contract.

Implemented and tested, but not yet selectable at boot: HashiCorp Vault Transit key custody, and post-quantum receipt signing with ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204) or hybrid Ed25519 + ML-DSA-65. The signing and verification paths and their tests exist; the deployment bootstrap accepts local and AWS KMS custody only. Do not buy a tier on the strength of these until they appear above this line.
